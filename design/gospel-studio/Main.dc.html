Gospel and neo-soul harmony

The twenty-two chords
a gospel player uses

Seven of them belong to the key. Four break it on purpose. Four more are borrowed from the parallel minor. Everything gospel and neo-soul harmony does is some arrangement of those twenty-two — and every one of them plays here, in any key, with the voicing a pianist would actually use.

Home the seven chords of the key 1–7
I Cmaj9 Home. The 9th on top keeps it from sounding like a hymn.
ii Dm9 The softest way to leave home. Half of all worship verses start here.
iii Em11 A tonic that floats. Substitute it for I when the vocal needs air.
IV Fmaj9♯11 The ♯11 is the single note that turns a church IV into a gospel one.
V G9sus Suspended, not dominant. It pulls home without sounding like a cadence.
vi Am11 The relative minor, stacked to four notes so it reads as colour, not sadness.
vii Bm7♭5 Rarely a destination. It is the first chord of the 7–3–6 turnaround.
